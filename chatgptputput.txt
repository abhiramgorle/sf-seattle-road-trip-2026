IMPORTANT NOTES
Pace: This is an ambitious trip with roughly 32–34 hours of driving. It is most comfortable if both travelers can drive.

Mt. Rainier: Sunrise and White River are currently closed because of the Grand Park 2 Fire. The plan intentionally uses Paradise. Recheck 48 hours before departure.

Mt. Hood: Active fire closures and extreme fire danger currently affect parts of the forest. Trillium Lake and Timberline are the core plan, but verify access before driving.

Crater Lake: East Rim construction prevents a complete Rim Drive loop; West Rim and North Entrance are currently open. Cleetwood Cove is closed for all of 2026.

Yosemite: No timed entry is required in 2026, but arrive early. Glacier Point is an optional swap for the Mist Trail only if you leave San Francisco around 3:30 a.m.; it adds about two hours.

Fuel: Do not let the tank fall below half near Yosemite, Redwood, Crater Lake, or Rainier. Cell service is unreliable; download offline maps and park maps.

Weather: Pack for hot Yosemite afternoons and cold, damp Redwood/Cascade mornings. Mountain visibility can change the value of photo stops.

BUDGET BREAKDOWN
Remaining budget: $1,700 total for two after the already-budgeted $300 rental.

Lodging ceiling: $700 total / $350 per person. Use the tent backups if cabin and motel totals exceed this.

Fuel: about $250 total / $125 per person, assuming roughly 1,550 miles and an efficient rental car.

Food: $360 total / $180 per person; groceries, packed breakfasts/lunches, and one casual dinner daily.

Passes and parking: about $100 total / $50 per person with the $80 resident annual pass, Trillium Lake fee, and small reservations/parking.

Adventure and contingency: $190 total / $95 per person.

Planned post-rental total: about $1,600 / $800 per person. With the $150 rental share, about $950 per person all-in.

Nonresident warning: If nobody qualifies for the $80 resident pass, the $250 nonresident annual pass raises the plan by $170 total. Offset it by using tent sites for two nights.
